FAQ

How is legal shooting light calculated?

From sunrise and sunset at your spot and your state's deer rule — 30 minutes before sunrise to 30 minutes after sunset in most states; Wisconsin (20 minutes after sunset), Connecticut (until sunset), South Carolina (one hour) and others are handled. Waterfowl uses the federal rule (½ hour before sunrise to sunset), and you can set custom hours. Official tables can differ by a minute or two and seasons vary, so always confirm your state's current regulations.

What are major and minor periods?

Solunar theory: majors are two hours centred on the moon overhead or underfoot, minors one hour centred on moonrise and moonset. They are strongest near new and full moon and when they line up with dawn or dusk.

Where does the weather come from?

The National Weather Service API (U.S. only): hourly temperature, wind and precipitation, plus the pressure trend from the nearest observation station. Tides come from the nearest NOAA CO-OPS station within 60 miles.

What is the activity index?

A 0–100 estimate for each hour combining dawn/dusk light, solunar periods, moon phase, pressure trend, wind, temperature swings, precipitation, the rut (deer) and tidal flow (fish). Tap any hour to see which factors raised or lowered it. It's a planning guide, not a guarantee.

What does iPhone Duo do?

Unfolded, the sky and dial sit on one page and the day's details on the other. Folded into a tent on your shooting rail, Stand Mode opens with a big countdown to the end of legal light.
